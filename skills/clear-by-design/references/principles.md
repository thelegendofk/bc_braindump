# Principles

The evidenced principles behind clear, understandable and pleasant documents. Each one with a
short statement, the "and what does that mean for the document", and the source. This makes it
possible to justify a design decision to a client instead of only asserting it.

---

## 1. Cognitive load and attention

**Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller).** Working memory is narrow. Load is of three kinds:
intrinsic (the matter itself), extraneous (bad presentation), germane (building up
understanding). Only the extraneous load can be lowered by design, and that is exactly the
lever. Means: remove everything that does not contribute to the message.

**Mayer, multimedia principles (selection).** Evidenced rules for how word and image work
together:
- Multimedia: word and image work better than word alone.
- Coherence: less is more, every superfluous word, image or decorative element does harm.
- Signaling: a cue (color, arrow, bold) guides the eye to what matters.
- Spatial Contiguity: text close to the image it belongs to, not in a legend far away.
- Redundancy: do not show the same text and read it out word for word at the same time.
- Segmenting: divide the material into digestible units, not as a block.
Means: one message per unit, label at the object, no double channel.

Sources: [Mayer, multimedia principles (DLI)](https://www.digitallearninginstitute.com/blog/mayers-principles-multimedia-learning),
[Multimedia Learning, UCSD](https://multimedia.ucsd.edu/best-practices/multimedia-learning.html).

## 2. Visual clarity and Gestalt

**Gestalt principles.** The eye groups automatically: proximity, similarity, enclosure,
connection, continuity. Means: design what belongs together close and alike, separate through
distance instead of through lines and boxes. White space is a tool, not empty space.

**Visual hierarchy.** Size, weight, color and position tell the reader what to read first. A
clear rank order leads the eye, a missing one forces a search. Means: one clear first point of
focus per slide or page.

Source: [Gestalt principles in data visualization](https://nastengraph.medium.com/gestalt-principles-in-data-visualization-a4e56e6074b5).

## 3. Data integrity and data visualization

**Tufte.** Keep the data ink ratio high, so maximize the ink that shows data and minimize
decorative ink. Chartjunk (3D, gradients, background images, superfluous grids) out. "Above
all else show the data." Graphical integrity: the area of the display matches the size of the
number, no distorted axes. Means: plain, honest charts beat decorated ones.

**Knaflic, Storytelling with Data.** First clarify whether a chart should explore or explain.
Guide the eye (one highlighted element, the rest calm). Lead with words, every chart has a
title, every axis a label. The addressee decides everything. Means: one chart makes one point,
and the point stands next to it as a sentence.

Sources: [Tufte principles](https://graficto.com/blog/discover-edward-tuftes-essential-principles-for-effective-data-visualization/),
[Knaflic, guiding principles](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/2017/8/9/my-guiding-principles).

## 4. Narrative and structure

**Answer-first, pyramid principle (Minto), BLUF.** The answer first, then the supports, then
the details. A reader short of time gets the result at once and reads the rest only if they
want to. Means: management summary and recommendation stand at the front and stand on their
own.

**Assertion-Evidence (Alley).** Every slide carries a message as a complete sentence in the
header, below it the visual piece of evidence, no topic heading with a bullet list. Evidenced:
better comprehension, better recall, clearly fewer misconceptions than with bullet slides.
Means: the slide title is a sentence, the body is an image or a chart.

**Narrative bracket.** A message with a beginning, tension and resolution sticks better than
loose facts. Means: starting position, conflict or question, solution, next step.

Sources: [Assertion-Evidence, Garner and Alley (ASEE)](https://peer.asee.org/assertion-evidence-slides-appear-to-lead-to-better-comprehension-and-recall-of-more-complex-concepts.pdf),
[What the research says about slide design](https://www.scottjallen.net/sjablog/assertion-evidence).

## 5. Behavioral design

**Kahneman, System 1 and System 2.** The larger part of decisions runs fast and intuitively
through System 1, not through slow analysis. Pure information and arguments often reach only
System 2, which is rarely active. Means: context, framing and light processing work more
strongly than one more argument.

**Framing and defaults.** How an option is framed and how it is preset steers the choice
strongly. Means: design the recommended option visibly as the obvious choice, choose the
reference value deliberately (anchoring), frame gains and costs honestly, but clearly.

**Cialdini, principles of persuasion.** Authority, social proof, scarcity, reciprocity,
consistency, liking, belonging are signals that System 1 uses as a shortcut. Means: place real
evidence for competence and for others who have taken the path visibly. Only true, never
invented.

**Thaler and Sunstein, Nudge and Sludge.** Sludge is any friction that makes a useful action
harder: complicated forms, unnecessary steps, hidden paths. A nudge makes the good choice
easier without forcing it. Means: reduce every call to action to one clear, easy step, run a
sludge check over every "we ask you to".

Sources: [Kahneman and Cialdini, System 1 and persuasion](https://ethicalpersuasion.com.au/2025/11/05/kahneman-and-cialdini/),
[Sludge, BehavioralEconomics.com](https://www.behavioraleconomics.com/resources/mini-encyclopedia-of-be/sludge/),
[Sunstein, Sludge Audits (Bloomberg Cities)](https://bloombergcities.jhu.edu/news/cass-sunstein-removing-sludge-gears-city-hall).

## 6. Fluency and reading pleasure

**Processing Fluency.** What is easy to process is understood better, judged more positively
and believed more readily. Light processing works like an unconscious trust cue. Evidenced for
financial disclosures (more readable texts trigger stronger reactions) and for readiness to
act (readable instructions are followed more often). Means: readability is not a luxury, it
raises agreement.

**Typographic readability.** Font size, contrast and spacing steer perceptual fluency.
Cramped, low-contrast or ornate typesetting lowers comprehension and impact. Means: font large
enough, high contrast, calm choice of typeface, clear line spacing.

**Aesthetic-Usability effect.** What looks beautiful is perceived as easier to use and more
credible. Means: clean design is not cosmetics, it pays into understandability and trust. That
is the evidenced core of "pleasant to read".

Sources: [Processing fluency and readability of disclosures (Harvard)](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2012/06/08/processing-fluency-and-investors-reactions-to-disclosure-readability/),
[Persuasive materials through processing fluency (BMC)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13104-017-2524-x),
[Disfluent typography and reading](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14606925.2020.1810434).

## Short version as reminders

- One message per unit, as a sentence, not as a topic.
- Answer first, then the reasoning.
- Remove everything that does not carry the message.
- Guide the eye, do not be equally loud everywhere.
- One chart, one point, honest axes.
- Make the good path the easy path, take out the friction.
- Readable is credible, beautiful is light.
