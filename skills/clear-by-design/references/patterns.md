# Patterns (before, after)

Concrete patterns for decks and reports, DE and EN. Each one shows the principle in use.
Before is the frequent wrong move, after is the evidenced alternative.

---

## Deck patterns

### 1. Slide title as a message, not as a topic (Assertion-Evidence)

Before (DE): title "Kostenübersicht", below it five bullets with numbers.
After (DE): title "Option A liefert den produktiven Kern für rund 46'000 CHF", below it a
plain cost chart. The title is the message, the chart is the proof.

Before (EN): title "Timeline", four bullets.
After (EN): title "The first production release is ready within twelve weeks", with a simple
milestone bar. The headline states the point, the visual proves it.

### 2. One message per slide

Before: one slide with architecture, costs and schedule at the same time.
After: three slides, each with one message. Segmenting lowers the load, every message stays.

### 3. Bullet wall to image plus core sentence

Before: eight bullets of running text on one slide, which the speaker reads out.
After: one core sentence at the top, below it a chart or a diagram. The speaker tells, the
slide shows. No double channel (Redundancy).

### 4. Guide the eye (Signaling)

Before: table, all rows alike, the reader searches for what matters.
After: the decisive row highlighted in color, the rest calm grey. One accent leads the eye.

### 5. Recommended option as default (framing)

Before: three options side by side with equal weight, the reader has to choose alone.
After: the recommended option marked visually as the obvious choice (frame, label
"empfohlen", named first), with one sentence why. Honest, the others stay visible.

### 6. Declutter the chart (Tufte, Knaflic)

Before: 3D bars, gradient, grid, legend far away from the bar.
After: flat bars, one highlighted bar, label directly at the bar, chart title as a message.
Data ink up, chartjunk out.

## Report patterns

### 7. Answer-first management summary (pyramid principle, BLUF)

Before (DE): the summary tells the procedure, the context and the method, the result comes on
page three.
After (DE): the first paragraph names the recommendation and the result, then follow the
supports. The reader short of time has the point at once.

Before (EN): opens with background and scope.
After (EN): opens with the recommendation in one sentence, then the reasons. Details stay
for readers who want them.

### 8. Signposting and intermediate messages

Before: long sections with topic headings ("Analyse", "Vorgehen").
After: headings as assertion sentences, one core sentence at the start of every section. The
reader can skim and understands the arc from the headings alone.

### 9. One thought per paragraph

Before: one paragraph with five thoughts, seven lines without air.
After: one thought per paragraph, three to five sentences, white space in between. Segmenting
and fluency.

### 10. Take the sludge out of the call to action

Before (DE): "Bitte prüfen Sie die Unterlagen, stimmen Sie sich intern ab, und melden Sie
uns bis Ende Monat zurück, welche der Optionen und unter welchen Bedingungen Sie
weiterverfolgen möchten."
After (DE): "Ein Entscheid genügt: Geben wir Option A frei? Eine kurze Rückmeldung bis
Freitag reicht." One step, clear, easy.

Before (EN): a paragraph asking the reader to review, consult, decide and reply with
conditions.
After (EN): "One decision is enough: shall we start with Option A? A short yes by Friday is
all we need." Friction removed.

### 11. Evidence where the decision is made (Cialdini)

Before: references in the appendix, the recommendation without evidence.
After: a matching reference or a piece of evidence directly next to the recommendation.
Authority and social proof work where the decision falls. Only real evidence.

### 12. Frame the number with a reference point (anchoring, honest)

Before: "Die Lösung kostet 46'000 CHF."
After: "Für rund 46'000 CHF, etwa die Kosten eines manuellen Prozessmonats, liefert die
Lösung den produktiven Kern." The number gets an honest reference point.

## Typography and white space (both formats)

- Font large enough, high contrast, calm type family (fluency).
- Generous line and paragraph spacing, white space as structure, not as leftover.
- Group what belongs together through proximity, separate through distance, not through lines.
- One clear hierarchy through size and weight, one deliberate accent per page.
- Consistent grid, calm alignment. Beautiful feels light, light feels credible.

## What does not count as a pattern

No dark pattern. No invented social proof, no hiding of disadvantages, no pressure.
Behavioral design here means making the good choice easy for the reader, transparent and in
their interest.
