# Proposal Structure, Client First

The order of a proposal is a persuasion decision, not a formatting one. The rule:
the client's world comes before your firm. Clients want to feel understood before
they want to understand you.

## The client-first flow

A generic consulting proposal structure, to adapt to the RFP or the house template
per mandate:

1. **Cover** and a **personal cover letter** signed by senior leadership, that
   references the specific conversation or RFP context and sets a personal tone.
2. **Executive summary** as the Situation-Complication-Resolution arc.
3. **Understanding your situation**: the client's challenges, goals, and context. A
   playback that proves you listened.
4. **Our solution approach**: the technical approach, architecture, and modular
   structure. Diagrams with action captions.
5. **Delivery plan and roadmap**: phases, milestones, and deliverables per phase.
   State the delivery model (for example a hybrid of agile build and classic
   controls) if the client values predictability.
6. **Your team**: named people, roles, and the relevant experience for this work.
7. **Why us**: three or four concrete differentiators, with a short firm
   introduction folded in as proof of capability.
8. **Commercial offer and next steps**: pricing, validity, and a clear next action.
9. **Appendices**: detailed CVs, case studies, reference projects, technical
   deep-dives.

## The key move: firm intro goes late

The most common structural mistake is to open with "who we are". Move the company
introduction to after the solution, inside or next to the Why us section. There it
reads as evidence that you can deliver what you just proposed, instead of a preamble
the client has to sit through before reaching their own problem.

## When the RFP mandates a structure

Follow the mandated structure exactly, compliance comes first. Apply the
client-first idea inside each mandated section instead: lead each section with the
client's need and the conclusion, not with your firm. The compliance matrix that
`shipley-proposal` produces tells you which requirement each section must answer.

## Adapt per mandate

The client-facing section list, the house boilerplate (company facts,
certifications, standard legal text), and the visual conventions are read per
mandate from the AGENTS.md, the style guide, or the house template. This skill does
not carry fixed company facts. Where the mandate points to a house template, start
from it and keep its section order unless there is a reason to change.

## Length and the pyramid

Keep the main body short and push detail to appendices. Apply the pyramid inside
each section, conclusion first. Typical targets, unless the RFP says otherwise:

- Word proposal: 12 to 15 pages in the main body, appendices as needed.
- Deck: 15 to 20 slides in the main body, appendices as needed.

A long main body is not thoroughness, it is a filter the reader has to fight
through. Thoroughness lives in the appendix.

## Consistency

Keep one visual system across the whole document and across Word and deck versions:
one color palette, one type system, consistent footers (for example client, project,
date, page), numbered sections, and a visual element on every page or slide. The
specific palette and fonts come from the house template via `inacta-proposal-deck`.
