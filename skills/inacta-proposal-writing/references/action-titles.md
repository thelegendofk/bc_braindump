# Action Titles

An action title is a full-sentence heading that states the takeaway of the
section or slide. A label names the topic. An action title makes the point. This
is the highest-leverage single change in the whole method, because senior
decision-makers skim, and action titles make the argument land even when the body
text is never read.

## The test

A reader who reads only the titles, in order, should understand the whole
proposal. If the titles read like a table of contents, they are labels, not action
titles.

## Rules

- Write a full sentence with a verb. A label has no verb.
- State the conclusion or the benefit, not the subject.
- Put a real number in the title wherever a real number exists. Numbers are
  memorable and are read first.
- Keep it to one line. If it needs two clauses, the second is usually the "so
  what", keep that and cut the rest.
- One message per title. If the section makes two points, it is probably two
  sections.
- Never put a number in a title that you cannot support in the body. An unsupported
  number is worse than no number.

## Before and after

| Label (weak) | Action title (strong) |
|---|---|
| Executive summary | A modular approach cuts claims processing time and keeps full regulatory compliance |
| Situation | Your three legacy systems each hold part of the customer record, which slows every claim |
| Our solution | One shared data layer removes the manual hand-offs between the three systems |
| Delivery approach | A three-phase model delivers the first working result in the first phase, not at the end |
| Project plan | Sprint reviews every two weeks let you correct course before cost is committed |
| Team | A senior lead who has delivered two comparable platforms owns the engagement end to end |
| Commercial offer | Phased pricing ties each payment to a delivered milestone |
| Why us | Deep sector knowledge plus hands-on delivery sets this apart from generalist consultancies |
| Risks | A phased approach with early reviews contains the main scope and integration risks |

## Working method

1. Write the section body first, or at least its main message.
2. Ask: what is the one thing the reader should take from this section?
3. Write that as a sentence. That is the title.
4. If a supporting number exists and is defensible, put it in the title.
5. Read all titles top to bottom. They should form a coherent argument on their
   own. Fix any that break the flow.

## For slides specifically

On a slide, the action title is the top line and the body is its proof. The chart,
table, or diagram supports the title. If the visual does not support the title,
either the title or the visual is wrong. Every figure also gets an action caption,
a full sentence stating what the reader should conclude from it.

## German note

Action titles work the same in German. Use full sentences with a verb, Swiss
spelling (ss not ß), and real umlauts. Avoid nominal-style headings ("Optimierung
der Prozesse"), write the point ("Ein gemeinsamer Datenlayer entfernt die manuellen
Übergaben zwischen den drei Systemen").
